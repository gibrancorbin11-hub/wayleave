A wayleave is the fee utilities have paid for 150 years for the right to run cables across private land.

Agents cross your app for free today. Every scrape, every RAG pull, every autonomous checkout runs through your infrastructure without paying the toll a human user implicitly pays in attention, ads, or a subscription.

Wayleave is the checkpoint at the property line — free middleware that tells you who's crossing, and an optional hosted meter that turns that knowledge into an invoice.

Shipping today

The free part — shipping today

Express/Node middleware, zero dependencies, native crypto only. It sorts every request into a lane:

verified_agent
Valid Ed25519 signature per the Web Bot Auth profile of RFC 9421
declared_agent
Says it's a bot, presents no signature
suspected_bot
Automation fingerprints — or a signature that fails verification
human
Browser-shaped. Never sees a paywall, ever.
402 Payment Required

On routes you price, non-human lanes get 402 Payment Required with an x402-shaped challenge.

// one line, any Express route
app.use(wayleave({ price: '$0.001' }));

The hosted meter

The paid part — what this list decides

The middleware emits a usage event for every priced request. Knowing what you're owed means keeping those events durably, deduplicating them, and turning them into something you can invoice against. That's the hosted meter:

ledger
Durable, idempotent record of every billable crossing
identity
Usage attributed to a cryptographic agent identity, not an IP guess
billing
Invoices and receipts per agent operator
directories
Key fetching, caching, and rotation handled for you

We never touch your money. Settlement stays on a payment rail you choose. Wayleave is the meter and the policy layer — you get billing records, not a middleman holding your funds.

Live & real

The middleware is real.

Signature verification is genuine Ed25519 over an RFC 9421 signature base. Forged keys, tampered requests, expired signatures, and replay-farming windows are all rejected, and it's tested adversarially. Sub-millisecond per request.

Not built yet

The hosted meter is not built.

Not a beta, not a private preview — unbuilt. This list is how I decide whether to build it. If enough people running real agent traffic want it, I build it. If not, I don't, and the middleware stays free and finished.

No launch date, no pricing page, no fake urgency. You'll get one email when there's something real, and nothing else.
