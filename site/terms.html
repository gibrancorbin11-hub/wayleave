What the software does and does not do

This is worth being explicit about, because the subject matter is security and money:

  • Wayleave is a screening and pricing layer, not a guarantee. It verifies signatures that are presented and classifies traffic on the evidence available. It cannot promise that every automated request is caught, nor that a determined party cannot imitate a browser.
  • Wayleave never takes custody of what agents pay you. The 402 challenge and the payment-proof check work. Where a settlement rail is configured, money moves from the agent to a third-party facilitator to an address you control; Wayleave is never a hop and never holds it. Wayleave is not a payment processor, money transmitter, or escrow service. Configuring a rail, and complying with the tax and regulatory rules that apply to money you collect, is yours to do.
  • Settlement depends on things outside our control. Whether any given agent can pay depends on that agent, on the facilitator you choose, and on networks nobody here operates. No amount of revenue is promised, projected, or guaranteed. The meter reports what crossed and what it would have been worth; it cannot make anyone pay.
  • Decisions it returns are advisory to your application. What you do with a lane classification — allow, deny, charge — is your configuration and your responsibility.

You are responsible for how you deploy it, what you charge, and for complying with the laws that apply to your own service.

The hosted meter — the paid part

The meter at meter.wayleave.dev is a subscription service. These terms apply if you start a plan.

Price, trial, and renewal

  • $49 per month, in US dollars, charged to the card you provide.
  • Every plan begins with a 3-day trial. A valid card is collected before the trial starts, and nothing is charged during those three days.
  • When the trial ends, the card is charged $49 and the subscription renews monthly until you cancel. If no valid payment method is on file when the trial ends, the subscription is cancelled rather than pursued for payment.
  • The price is flat and based on metered events. It is never a percentage of anything agents pay you — taking a cut of that flow would make this a payment intermediary, which it deliberately is not.
  • Prices may change, but not for an existing subscription without notice by email first. You can cancel before any change takes effect.
  • Signing in does not start a plan and does not ask for a card. The ledger runs in observe-only mode for as long as you want.

Cancelling and refunds

  • You can cancel at any time from Account → Manage billing, which opens a portal where you can cancel, change your card, and see past invoices. No email or phone call is required.
  • Cancelling stops the next renewal. Your plan stays active until the end of the period you have already paid for.
  • Cancel during the 3-day trial and you are never charged at all.
  • Monthly charges are not pro-rated on cancellation. If something went wrong on our side — an outage, a duplicate charge, a bill you did not expect — email and it gets refunded. That is a commitment, not a discretion.

What a plan gets you, and what it does not

A plan gets you the hosted ledger: durable, deduplicated records of billable crossings, attribution by cryptographic identity, CSV export, and key directory handling. It is provided on a reasonable-efforts basis. There is no uptime SLA, and none should be inferred from these terms or from anything on this website.

A plan does not get you revenue. What agents pay you depends on the agents, your prices, and your settlement rail — see above. Nothing here is a projection or promise of earnings.

Suspension

An account may be suspended for non-payment, or for use that attacks the service or other customers. Where the reason is non-payment, you get notice and a chance to fix the card first.
